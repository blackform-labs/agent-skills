# Spacing by relationship

Start with the two things being separated. An icon and its label, two adjacent cards, and a card group and the following section have different owners and may use different values. Identify the component variant, density, state, writing direction and available width. Compare like edges on the same axis.

For a Material component, use its [component sheet](../components/index.md) and the corresponding official Specs section. For a layout decision, use the product's named spacing role and the layout intent below. Atlassian and Carbon offer examples where the product leaves room for a choice. Their values become testable product expectations when the project adopts them. NN/g explains how people perceive relationships and supplies no numeric spacing threshold.

## Traceability matrix

The IDs identify the source family and rule. The source register below gives the original pages and sections. **High** means a deterministic comparison is possible once the target, units and applicable rule are confirmed. **Partial** means geometry or implementation can be checked but the interpretation needs review. **Human** means someone must judge the relationship in context.

| A1 · Token selection | M3 foundation, recommendation | Trace the component attribute through its system role or reference token to the resolved value. The Material spacing baseline is 8 dp (`md.sys.measurement.space100`), with smaller steps including 2, 4 and 6 dp. The published spacing system tokens target Jetpack Compose. A web project can map the design intent to its own tokens. | Inspect the declaration and resolved token in the active theme. Establish the dp-to-platform mapping before measuring. | High with a known token graph and mapping. A resolved number alone cannot establish provenance. |
| A2 · Icon, label and input | M3 component, variant-specific measurements | Use the actual control's icon-to-label or icon-to-text gap. For filled and outlined text fields with icons, the published icon-to-text gap is 16 dp. Supporting text starts with 4 dp top padding. The external space to the next field belongs to the form. For a button, select its size in the [button sheet](../components/buttons.md). | Measure the correct slots, outer field inset and supporting-text box separately. Exercise empty, populated and error states as applicable. [Text fields](../components/text-fields.md) | High for identified boxes. Optical distance between the artwork and glyphs requires review. |
| A3 · Card interior, neighboring cards and next section | M3 component, lateral inset and inter-card maximum | Apply the [card measurements](../components/cards.md) to the specified sides and to adjacent cards in one group. For vertical card content, identify the component slot or product token. Give the next independent section its own layout gap. | Mark the group boundary first. Measure side inset, internal vertical gap, edge-to-edge inter-card gap and outer section gap independently. | High for geometry after grouping is confirmed. Partial for automatically inferred groups. |
| A4 · Repeated list rows | M3 component, variant-specific defaults and measurements | Select the [list variant](../components/lists.md) by anatomy and line count. Distinguish row height, internal slots, dividers and gaps between rows. A wrapped description can legitimately increase a row's height. | Compare equivalent rows and aligned leading/content edges. Inspect a multiline row and any custom action's target separately. | High for named dimensions. Partial for visual rhythm. |
| A5 · Page margins, grid gutters and columns | Android platform standard and M3 layout recommendation | On compact Android layouts, 16 dp is the standard side margin for body content and actions. Allow separately for system insets. The gutter lies **between** columns. Android illustrates four mobile columns. Choose column count, tracks and margins for available space and content hierarchy. A full-bleed image can extend beyond the body alignment. | Overlay the grid at compact and larger widths. Measure from the body boundary to its first column and between adjacent tracks. Check action visibility around system UI. | High for declared grid geometry. Partial for choosing the grid. |
| A6 · Navigation | M3 component, adaptive recommendation | A navigation bar suits compact or medium windows. A rail suits medium and larger windows. Medium permits either. Keep destinations identifiable when navigation changes form. Use the chosen variant's [bar](../components/navigation-bar.md) or [rail](../components/navigation-rail.md) sheet for its internal spacing. | Resize across the product's switch point. Check the selected destination, wrapped labels, icon/label pairing and available body width. | Partial. Rendered bounds and selected state can be tested. Recognition needs visual review. |
| A7 · Responsive composition | Android platform and M3 foundation, recommendation | Fit the task to the window. A list and detail can occupy separate views when compact and sit side by side when space permits. Change column spans, reflow controls or cap content width when stretching would obscure the groups. | Follow the same item or action through both layouts and just across the transition. Inspect long copy, hidden supporting panes and the route back. | Partial. Bounds, overflow and navigation can be tested at selected widths. |
| A8 · Padding, gaps and density | M3 foundation, recommendation with customization | Follow [spacing ownership](layout.md#spacing-ownership). Choose component padding for edge-to-content space, parent gaps for siblings and layout margins for outer boundaries. Map density to the affected attribute. M3 recommends retaining spacing by default when text scales to 200%. | Inspect parent and child declarations, then measure repeated, single-child and enlarged-text states. Check wrapping and targets. | High for box geometry at tested settings. Partial for legibility. |
| B1 · Compact details, roomy interiors and sections | Atlassian design-system recommendation | The three usage bands below suggest candidate tokens. Match the relationship and density. A title/description pair in a compact card can use the small band. A roomier card can use the medium band. The large band is a candidate for page-level separation. | Check the project's mapping, the gap actually rendered and nearby groups at the same density. | High for an adopted token. Human for choosing a band. |
| B2 · Similar items and responsive alignment | Atlassian design-system recommendation and grid defaults | Give equivalent list or table items a consistent pattern. An optical correction may compensate for unequal visual weight. Atlassian's main-content grid varies gutters and margins by viewport, with local overrides possible. | Compare peers with the same role. Inspect grid tracks and margins across a breakpoint. Check an optical adjustment in the rendered layout. | Partial. Geometry can identify outliers. |
| C1 · Heading and description | NN/g usability principle | A heading should read with the content it introduces. Give that pair a closer visual relationship than the preceding block when whitespace carries the grouping. Consider type, boundaries and line height. | Inspect the heading with both its neighbors, including a wrapping description. Ask which block the heading names. | Human. Pairwise distances identify candidates for review. |
| C2 · Label, help, adjacent fields and field groups | NN/g usability principle | Keep a top-aligned label visually attached to its own input. Separate successive field pairs and meaningful groups such as shipping and billing. With persistent borders or an embedded label, evaluate the additional grouping cue. | Inspect the two nearest inputs and labels. Trigger a wrapping help or error message and check which input it appears to describe. | Human for perceived ownership. Automation can compare bounds and verify programmatic labels separately. |
| C3 · Responsive proximity | NN/g usability principle | Keep controls near the content they affect and related alternatives easy to compare after reflow. A column that stacks between a heading and its action can break their apparent relationship. | Compare wide and narrow arrangements, visual order and distance to competing items. Follow the user's task in each arrangement. | Human. Reflow snapshots document the evidence. |
| D1 · Component padding and spacing scale | Carbon design-system recommendation | Choose the adopted token for the component edge or relationship. Smaller increments address detail, larger ones can control density. Fluid centering, automatic free space and grid gutters have their own roles. | Trace the selected token and measure each applicable edge. Identify fluid layout rules before testing for a fixed value. | High for an adopted fixed mapping. |
| D2 · Stacking siblings | Carbon design-system recommendation | Let the horizontal or vertical Stack own uniform sibling spacing. Keep internal padding with each child. A custom gap is supported. | Compare first, middle and last edges. Remove or wrap a child and check for doubled gaps or stray end spacing. | High for a known stack's geometry. |
| D3 · Breakpoints, density and hierarchy | Carbon design-system recommendation and option | A Carbon spacing token has a fixed definition. A layout may select another step at a breakpoint. A dense group can share a compact rhythm while a heading or section receives more surrounding space. | Inspect the token selection and rendered layout on both sides of the breakpoint. Review whether the hierarchy survives. | High for declared steps. Human for hierarchy. |

## Atlassian scale

The numeric suffix gives the percentage of an 8 px base. The published conversions below use a 16 px rem base. Resolve rem against the actual root font size during review. Each row lists corresponding entries in order. [Spacing, scale and usage](https://atlassian.design/foundations/spacing/)

| Usage band | Tokens | px | rem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small · compact parts | `space.0`, `space.025`, `space.050`, `space.075`, `space.100` | 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 | 0, .125, .25, .375, .5 |
| Medium · larger or less dense parts | `space.150`, `space.200`, `space.250`, `space.300` | 12, 16, 20, 24 | .75, 1, 1.25, 1.5 |
| Large · page layout | `space.400`, `space.500`, `space.600`, `space.800`, `space.1000` | 32, 40, 48, 64, 80 | 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5 |

For intentional overlap or bleed, Atlassian also defines `space.negative.` with suffixes `025`, `050`, `075`, `100`, `150`, `200`, `250`, `300`, `400`, respectively −2, −4, −6, −8, −12, −16, −20, −24, −32 px. Its guide suggests considering the Bleed primitive first. [All tokens, Space](https://atlassian.design/components/tokens/all-tokens)
